I. The Misdirection

At what frame rate does authorship become ceremonial?

Not "will machines make art" — they already do, daily, on every feed. The question is narrower and harder: which human, with what authority, can still say no to a shipped artifact, how fast, and what happens when the answer arrives after the premiere?

Three slides, and only three. In the loop: the human decides every cut; the machine executes. A cel painter in 1988 inking a smear frame she chose. On the loop: the machine nominates, the human approves — a dubbing mixer at 02:00 approving the tenth "final" stem that night, ears fatigued, pressing green because red means another hour. Told afterwards: the machine ships, the human reads the credits. Everything in this book is an attempt to date, per rung and per artifact, which slide we are on — because studios claim the first, practice lives in the second, and the third arrives without announcement.

The wall and the playground

The twentieth-century futurist consensus made one bet, twice, from opposite sides of the table. The evangelists (Kurzweil 2005, Moravec) promised heavy matter transformed: molecular assembly, abundant fusion, orbital industry. The catastrophists (Bostrom 2014, Yudkowsky) warned of a cold optimizer turning heavy matter into paperclips or computronium. Both placed the singularity in physics. Both treated art, narrative, pathos — the things that make apes weep at arranged light — as the last redoubt of biology, guarded by qualia, mortality, soul.

They inverted the physics.

Moving concrete is slow. Smelting titanium is slow. Permits are slow. Fatigue is real: vocal folds hemorrhage, hands cramp, studios go bankrupt drawing 24 frames per second at peak intensity for 100 minutes (Akira, 1988; Redline, 2009 — cites in Issue #5). The speed of light is not negotiable and neither is payroll.

Semantic space has no such drag. A latent step costs matrix multiplication. A har­monic shift costs nothing to re-render. A face costs nothing to re-interpolate once the encoder exists. The aesthetic singularity did not need to solve thermodynamics. It needed the complete map of the keyboard — and five millennia of human civilization had obligingly published it: poetics, counterpoint, chiaroscuro manuals, screenwriting beats, honkaku deduction rules, melodrama cue sheets. An unauthenticated interface, scraped at scale.

So the machine did not arrive as an accountant. It arrived as a kami with perfect pitch.

What this book will not do

Stated here, absolute, before any facts: no likeness-targeted clone guides, no filter- or watermark-defeat detail, no CSAM-adjacent description, no code. The weekly output of song-stack models makes this constraint load-bearing rather than decorative — the components are commercial, the pipelines are documented, and a book that teaches would be a manual with a thesis stapled on. Institutional facts (who shipped what, when, under whose authority) and governance criteria (what gate, owned by whom, verified how). A paragraph that helps an operator press an accelerator gets cut no matter how true it is. See CONTRIBUTING.md Rule 2 — the one rule in this trilogy that is not up for debate.

Fleet link

On-hyperwar liquidated the Pentagon's command loops one approval gate at a time. On-ai-takeover liquidated the $50/seat SaaS moat one dependency at a time. This book liquidates the studio's compromise envelope one trade-off at a time. Same math, other domain: reversibility lost in small dated steps, each defensible alone, terminal together.
